ASCII Dioramas

Miniature 3D worlds rendered as coloured monospace glyphs with a tilt-shift blur, seen from high above through a long lens.

Scenes fill any screen, phone to ultrawide. S plays each scene's own polyrhythmic music. Space pauses. Add ?fox=still, ?fox=off, ?tilt=off, ?title=off, ?t=12 (frozen at 12 s), ?q=low (quality: high, mid, low) or ?res=phone (also tablet, laptop, desktop, 4k, ultrawide, 1280x720) to any scene's address.

The same engine, pointed at a photograph instead of a 3D scene. Each pixel is sorted into a surface (sky, water, foliage, stone, wall, warm colours) from its colour and texture, then redrawn as glyphs with the same tilt-shift lens.

  1. Open it with the test photo, a Venice waterfront at sunset, which comes with a depth map: the picture sways gently in 3D and the blur follows real distance.
  2. Use Choose image in its corner, or drop an image file on the page, to see your own picture as glyphs. It is read on your device; nothing is uploaded.
  3. Click Add depth to estimate its depth with Depth Anything V2, run in your browser. The model is about 50 MB, downloaded once; after that each picture takes a fraction of a second on a GPU, a few seconds without one.

Flags: ?img=<url> loads another picture, ?depth=auto estimates depth on load, ?depth=off shows it flat, ?view=classes shows the surfaces it guessed.

About

Started in November 2026 as a side project inside Curiositas, after seeing ASCII diorama demos circulating online—in particular the work by @HelloVyom and @dhruvtwt_ (repo).

Each scene is a short main.js on a shared engine: it shapes the land, places instanced grass, flowers, trees and props, and renders them into a half-float target. A glyph shader then redraws every cell as a character chosen by surface and brightness, and a tilt-shift pass blurs the top and bottom like a miniature photograph. The camera sweeps slowly back and forth while a small creature walks through.
